How these numbers are worked out

This tool follows the method ASIC's Moneysmart describes for Australian home loans: "Interest on most home loans is calculated daily; each day, your lender subtracts your offset account balance from your loan balance before calculating interest." (moneysmart.gov.au, mortgage offset accounts). It is an estimate, not your lender's figure.

  • Interest accrues daily on the balance minus your offset, at the annual rate ÷ 365, and is charged monthly in arrears — the standard Australian variable-loan convention.
  • Fortnightly repayments use the "half-monthly" convention by default (fortnightly payment = monthly payment ÷ 2), because that is what Australian lenders mean by fortnightly repayments and what produces the well-known payoff saving. A true-fortnightly reamortisation option sits behind the Advanced toggle and is labelled there.
  • Repayments are rounded to the cent; because daily accrual on a 365-day basis charges slightly more than the monthly-compounded quoted payment, a small final payment settles the balance exactly at zero.
  • Moneysmart's own basic mortgage calculator simplifies by compounding at your repayment frequency rather than daily. We model daily accrual because that is how lenders actually charge, and because it is the only honest way to model an offset. Expect small differences against that calculator; both are estimates.
  • Not modelled: fees, LMI, stamp duty, redraw rules, interest-only periods, honeymoon rates, repayment holidays, or rate changes your lender makes on their own.
  • Your interest rate is whatever you type in — we do not fetch or forecast rates.

Different lenders round, accrue and charge on slightly different days, so a small difference against your bank's statement is normal and expected. If your figure differs materially, your lender's is the correct one.
